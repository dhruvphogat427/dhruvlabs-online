Face shape guide

What is my face shape? How to tell, and what suits it

There are six common face shapes: oval, round, square, oblong, heart and diamond. You can find yours in about two minutes with a mirror and a soft tape measure. Then use the sections below to pick a haircut, beard and glasses that work with your shape.

OvalLonger than wide, soft jaw RoundAs wide as long, soft jaw SquareAs wide as long, strong jaw OblongLong, even widths HeartWide forehead, narrow chin DiamondWide cheekbones, narrow ends

How to find your face shape at home

Pull your hair back, look straight at a mirror and keep a relaxed, neutral face. Take four measurements and write them down.

  1. Forehead: measure across the widest part of your forehead, about halfway between your eyebrows and your hairline.
  2. Cheekbones: measure across your face at the widest point of your cheekbones, just below the outer corners of your eyes.
  3. Jaw: measure from the tip of your chin to the corner of your jaw below the ear, then double it.
  4. Face length: measure from the middle of your hairline straight down to the tip of your chin.

Now compare. Divide your face length by your widest width. That ratio, and which of the three widths is biggest, decide your shape.

Many faces fall between two shapes. If you are close to two, read both sections below. A tape measure is fine for a rough answer, but small differences matter, which is why an app that measures hundreds of points is more reliable than eyeballing a photo.

The best haircut, beard and glasses for each face shape

The idea is always the same: add what your shape lacks and avoid stretching what it already has. A long face needs width, a round face needs height and angles, a strong jaw needs softness. For more detail, see the full guides to haircuts, beard styles and glasses for each face shape.

Oval face

Balanced proportions, longer than wide, rounded jaw.

An oval face is the most flexible shape. Most haircuts work, so choose by your hair type and how much styling you want.

Haircuts

Almost anything: textured crops, side parts, quiffs, long layers, bobs and fringes all sit well.

Beards

Most styles work. A short boxed beard or light stubble keeps the natural balance.

Glasses

Most frames work. Pick frames about as wide as the widest part of your face.

Go easy on: heavy fringes that cover the forehead completely, which hide the balance that makes oval faces easy to style.

Round face

About as wide as long, full cheeks, soft jaw.

The goal is to add length and angles so the face looks longer and more defined.

Haircuts

Height on top with shorter sides: quiffs, pompadours, faux hawks. For longer hair, long layers and a side part.

Beards

Beards that are longer at the chin, like an extended goatee or a short boxed beard kept tight on the cheeks.

Glasses

Square, rectangular or angular frames that add structure.

Go easy on: blunt, chin-length cuts and round frames, which repeat the roundness.

Square face

About as wide as long, strong and angular jaw.

A square face already has strong structure. The aim is a little softness and a little length.

Haircuts

Textured, softer cuts: messy crops, side-swept fringes, layered styles, a classic side part.

Beards

Rounded beards and stubble that soften the jaw corners, or a beard slightly longer at the chin.

Glasses

Round or oval frames that contrast with the angles.

Go easy on: very boxy beards and sharp, flat-topped cuts, which make the face look wider.

Oblong (long) face

Clearly longer than wide, with even forehead, cheekbone and jaw widths.

An oblong face needs width, not height. Keep volume at the sides and avoid adding length.

Haircuts

Textured fringes, side parts and medium cuts with some fullness at the sides.

Beards

Short at the chin and fuller at the sides, like a short boxed beard. This makes a long face look shorter.

Glasses

Tall, bold frames, such as square or browline frames, that break up the length.

Go easy on: tall pompadours and quiffs, very short buzz cuts, and long pointed goatees, which all add length.

Heart face

Wide forehead, narrower jaw, pointed chin.

Balance a wider forehead by adding fullness lower down.

Haircuts

Side-swept fringes and medium lengths with fullness around the jaw; for longer hair, layers from the chin down.

Beards

A fuller beard or chin beard that adds width to a narrow jaw.

Glasses

Frames lighter on top or wider at the bottom, like rimless or round frames.

Go easy on: lots of volume on top and very short sides, which widen the forehead further.

Diamond face

Wide cheekbones, narrower forehead and jaw.

Add fullness at the forehead and chin so they balance the cheekbones.

Haircuts

Fringes and textured tops that add width at the forehead; chin-length layers for longer hair.

Beards

A fuller chin, like a short full beard or a goatee, to widen the jaw line.

Glasses

Oval or browline frames with detail on top.

Go easy on: slicked-back styles with tight sides, which make the cheekbones look wider.

Common questions

What is the most common face shape?

Oval and oblong faces are very common, and many faces sit between two shapes. That is why measuring beats guessing from a photo: the numbers show which shape you are closest to.

How do I know if I have an oval face?

An oval face is about one and a half times as long as it is wide, the forehead is a little wider than the jaw, and the jawline is rounded rather than square. If your length is close to your width, you are more likely round or square.

Can a face shape be between two shapes?

Yes. Many people are, for example, between oval and oblong. Pick the shape your measurements are closest to, then use the advice for both.

Does my face shape change when I gain or lose weight?

It can shift a little, mostly around the cheeks and jaw. The bone structure that sets your forehead width and face length stays the same, so the overall shape usually does too.
