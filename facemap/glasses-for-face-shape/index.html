Glasses guide

The best glasses for your face shape

Glasses work by contrast. Angular frames give a soft face structure; round frames soften a strong one. Pick your face shape below for the frame shapes that suit it, then check the size so they actually fit.

The one rule: contrast, not repetition

Frames that repeat your face shape exaggerate it: round frames make a round face rounder, boxy frames make a square jaw squarer. Frames with the opposite shape balance it.

Size matters as much as shape. The frame should be about as wide as the widest part of your face, with your eyes near the centre of each lens.

Glasses for each face shape

Glasses for an oval face

Longer than wide, balanced, rounded jaw.

Most frames suit an oval face. Focus on size and keep the balance.

Best frames

Rectangular, square, wayfarer-style, aviator or geometric frames all work.

Size

Pick frames about as wide as the widest part of your face, or slightly wider.

Good to know

This is the shape where bold colours and statement frames are easiest to wear.

Go easy on: oversized frames that cover the cheekbones and hide the face's balance.

Glasses for a round face

About as wide as long, full cheeks, soft jaw.

Add structure with angles and a frame slightly wider than the face.

Best frames

Rectangular, square, browline or geometric frames with clear corners.

Size

Slightly wider than your face, and not too deep, so the face looks longer.

Good to know

A higher bridge and darker top rim lift the eye upwards.

Go easy on: round frames and small frames, which repeat the roundness and make the face look wider.

Glasses for a square face

About as wide as long, strong and angular jaw.

Soften a strong jaw with curves.

Best frames

Round, oval, aviator or rimless frames.

Size

Narrow to medium depth; avoid frames deeper than the distance to your cheekbones.

Good to know

Thin metal frames soften the face more than thick acetate.

Go easy on: boxy, square frames with sharp corners, which make the jaw look heavier.

Glasses for an oblong (long) face

Clearly longer than wide, even widths.

Break up the length with deeper frames and detail at the sides.

Best frames

Tall square frames, browline frames, oversized frames or frames with decorated temples.

Size

Deeper lenses that cover more of the face from top to bottom.

Good to know

A low bridge makes the nose, and the face, look shorter.

Go easy on: small, narrow frames, which make the face look even longer.

Glasses for a heart-shaped face

Wide forehead, narrower jaw, pointed chin.

Balance a wider forehead with frames that are lighter on top or wider at the bottom.

Best frames

Rimless, semi-rimless, round, oval or aviator frames.

Size

About as wide as your forehead, not wider.

Good to know

Light colours and thin rims keep attention away from the forehead.

Go easy on: heavy browline frames and frames with decoration on top, which widen the forehead.

Glasses for a diamond face

Wide cheekbones, narrower forehead and jaw.

Soften wide cheekbones and add width at the brow.

Best frames

Oval, cat-eye, browline or rimless frames.

Size

As wide as your cheekbones, with detail on the top rim.

Good to know

Frames with a strong brow line balance a narrow forehead.

Go easy on: narrow, small frames, which make the cheekbones look wider.

Check the size before you buy

Most frames have three numbers printed inside the arm, for example 52-18-140: the lens width (52 mm), the bridge width (18 mm) and the arm length (140 mm). If a pair you already own fits well, use its numbers as your starting point.

  • Width: the frame should be about as wide as your face at the temples; no big gap and no squeezing.
  • Eyes: your pupils should sit roughly in the middle of each lens.
  • Brows: the top rim should follow your eyebrows, not sit far above or cut through them.
  • Bridge: the frame should rest on your nose without pinching or sliding down.

Sunglasses follow the same rules

The same contrast rules apply to sunglasses. Aviators suit oval, square and heart faces; wayfarer and square styles suit round and oval faces; oversized frames suit long faces.

Common questions

What glasses suit a round face?

Angular frames such as rectangular, square, browline or geometric frames, a little wider than the face. Avoid small round frames.

What glasses suit an oval face?

Most frames suit an oval face. Choose frames about as wide as the widest part of your face.

What glasses are best for a big nose or long face?

Frames with a lower bridge and deeper lenses, such as tall square or browline frames, shorten a long face and nose.

Do face shape rules apply to sunglasses?

Yes. The same contrast rules work: angular frames for soft faces, curved frames for angular faces, and deeper frames for long faces.
