Beard guide

The best beard style for your face shape

A beard changes the shape of your face. Length at the chin makes a face look longer; fullness at the sides makes it look wider. Pick your face shape below to see which beard styles suit it and how to trim them.

The one rule: put the hair where your face needs it

Think of your beard as a way to redraw your jawline. Hair on the chin adds length, hair on the cheeks and jaw adds width, and the shape of the corners decides whether the jaw looks soft or sharp.

So a round face wants length at the chin and tight sides, a long face wants the opposite, and a square jaw wants its corners rounded off. The cheek line and neckline you choose matter as much as the style itself.

Beard styles for each face shape

Beard styles for an oval face

Longer than wide, balanced, rounded jaw.

Most beards work on an oval face. Keep the balance rather than changing it.

Best styles

Short boxed beard, heavy stubble, a corporate beard, or a well-kept full beard.

How to trim

Keep the cheek line natural and the neckline about one to two fingers above the Adam's apple.

Good to know

Pick by how your beard grows and how much upkeep you want, not by shape.

Go easy on: very long, wide beards that make the lower half of the face look heavy.

Beard styles for a round face

About as wide as long, full cheeks, soft jaw.

Add length at the chin and keep the sides short so the face looks longer and more defined.

Best styles

Extended goatee, Van Dyke, anchor beard, or a boxed beard left longer at the chin.

How to trim

Use a shorter guard on the cheeks than on the chin, and keep a sharp, slightly low cheek line.

Good to know

A pointed or squared-off chin adds the angles a round face lacks.

Go easy on: full, round beards with bushy sides and mutton chops, which add width.

Beard styles for a square face

About as wide as long, strong and angular jaw.

A square jaw is already strong. Soften the corners and add a little length at the chin.

Best styles

Rounded full beard, circle beard, balbo, or heavy stubble.

How to trim

Round off the jaw corners and keep the sides a little shorter than the chin.

Good to know

Light stubble shows off a strong jaw if you want to keep it visible.

Go easy on: boxy beards with sharp corners and thin chinstraps that trace the jaw angles.

Beard styles for an oblong (long) face

Clearly longer than wide, even widths.

A long face needs width. Keep the chin short and let the sides fill out.

Best styles

Short boxed beard, mutton chops, heavy stubble, or a chevron moustache with short stubble.

How to trim

Keep the chin shorter than the sides and the cheek line fairly high.

Good to know

A moustache breaks up the length of the face nicely.

Go easy on: long goatees and long, pointed beards, which make the face look even longer.

Beard styles for a heart-shaped face

Wide forehead, narrower jaw, pointed chin.

Add fullness at the jaw and chin to balance a wider forehead and a narrow chin.

Best styles

Full beard, Garibaldi, or a short full beard that fills out the jaw and chin.

How to trim

Keep more length and bulk at the jaw and chin than on the upper cheeks.

Good to know

If the cheeks grow thin, a full chin beard still adds the width you need.

Go easy on: pencil-thin chinstraps and pointed styles that make the chin look narrower.

Beard styles for a diamond face

Wide cheekbones, narrower forehead and jaw.

Widen the chin and soften the cheekbones with fullness lower down.

Best styles

Full beard, short boxed beard, or a goatee with a fuller chin.

How to trim

Keep the chin full and the cheek line natural rather than cut high and sharp.

Good to know

A fuller chin balances wide cheekbones better than a longer one.

Go easy on: sharply faded sides and pointed beards, which make the cheekbones look wider.

If your beard is patchy

  • Give it time: let it grow for four to six weeks before judging. Gaps often fill in.
  • Go shorter: heavy stubble of about 3 to 5 mm hides patches better than a long beard.
  • Use what grows: if the cheeks are thin, a goatee, Van Dyke or moustache keeps the hair where it is fullest.
  • Keep lines clean: a tidy neckline and cheek line makes any beard look intentional.

Trimming basics

  • Neckline: about one to two fingers above the Adam's apple, curving up behind the jaw to the ears.
  • Cheek line: follow the natural line, or set it a little lower for a cleaner look.
  • Guards: use a shorter guard on the sides and a longer one on the chin to add length; the reverse adds width.

Common questions

What beard style suits a round face?

Styles that add length at the chin and keep the sides short: an extended goatee, Van Dyke, anchor beard or a boxed beard left longer at the chin.

What is the best beard for a square jaw?

A rounded full beard, circle beard or balbo that softens the corners. Heavy stubble also works if you want to show off the jaw.

Which beard makes a face look slimmer?

A beard with short sides and more length at the chin draws the face downwards and makes it look slimmer.

How long should I grow my beard before choosing a style?

Around four to six weeks. That is enough to see how it fills in on the cheeks and chin.
